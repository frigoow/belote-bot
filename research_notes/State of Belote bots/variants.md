# Contrée, Coinche and regional Belot bots

## What has been achieved for French contract-bidding variants?

### Takeaway

Colver is the most complete public, runnable technical reference found for a four-suit Contrée variant. It offers a learned bidder, a fast learned card player, and a slower search alternative with a learned joint distribution over hidden hands. Research evidence for Deep CFR is substantially weaker and a public cross-project league or independently controlled human benchmark remains unavailable.

### Cited Findings

- Colver couples an MIT Rust core and WASM solver with Python bindings, a local arena, rules, configurable bot specs, and downloadable model weights. Standard web bot Dédé runs information-set double-dummy (IS-DD): sample worlds constrained by public cards, void suits and trump ceilings, solve each by alpha-beta, and aggregate scores. The 10.7M-parameter Playgen v2 causal transformer (Bumbelote on the website) generates joint completions from the visible hand, auction, and play sequence, with uniform legal-world fallback. Website search budget is one second. Its CPU implementation and public weights can reproduce the distribution offline, with a large speed penalty. Web model service is private. [Engine](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver#agents-ia) [Playgen model card](https://huggingface.co/Avo-k/colver-playgen-v2) [Deployment](https://colver.net/about)
- DouDou50, <1 ms on the repo benchmark, uses a dueling residual Q-network and 50 million card-play training steps with the neural bidder fixed. The authors describe it as strongest overall on the live site, and list Dédé as the strongest search-based option in the repo. Avoid implying Dédé universally beats DouDou or vice versa. Colver's full historical arena results are private. Its score-aware V6 bidder (Calzone), trained over 75M steps on actual results from IS-DD and match score, wins 55.8% against its predecessor V5 with Deep Monte Carlo card play fixed, and 57.3% when card play is fixed to IS-DD. The summary gives no sample size or intervals for these two numbers. Solo web play against DouDou is account free. [Engine](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver#agents-ia) [Live site](https://colver.net/) [Agent overview](https://colver.net/about)
- Colver's rule set uses four suit contracts, a bid of 80–160 or capot, coinche and surcoinche. It uses unrounded points-made plus contract, with ×2 and ×3 on the contract alone. That is a specific ruleset, including a different scoring convention from the federation's Coinche rules. FFB publishes separate Contrée and Coinche regulations (declarations, optional All Trump/No Trump, and scoring differ). [Colver rules](https://colver.net/regles) [Official Contrée](https://www.ffbelote.org/documents/regles-officielles-de-la-Belote-Contree-27-01-2016.pdf) [Official Coinche](https://www.ffbelote.org/documents/REGLES-DE-LA-BELOTE-COINCHEE.pdf)
- Sophia Günlük and Théo Saulus's UdeM/Mila project report follows an FFB Contrée variant with points annoncés and models the auction as a sequence of calls. The reported Deep CFR variant sampled too aggressively to learn strong play and converged in early self-play to all-pass, termed a trivial equilibrium by the authors in their setup. Against hand-coded opponents its attack strategies oscillated. PPO learned some bidding precision and around 70% defensive success against the stronger heuristic, a conditional stat that is not an overall win rate. The study leaves whole-match target scoring outside its experiments. AGPL-3.0 source. [Project report, sections 2, 4–6](https://theosaulus.fr/assets/pdf/Adversarial_ML_ProjectReport.pdf) [Source](https://github.com/theosaulus/coinche)
- Eric Picot's original gym-coinche is an older baseline; author reported performance slightly but significantly above random. Two Stanford 2023 class projects can be identified by title through Günlük and Saulus's bibliography; Stanford says its report files need a Stanford email, and the project report describes both as simplifying bidding. [Picot source](https://github.com/EricPicot/gym-coinche) [Picot's retrospective](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/eric-picot-757b6b105_english-below-il-y-a-5-ans-lors-dune-activity-7285653405937602560-PrOg) [Stanford class index](https://aa228.stanford.edu/old-projects/) [Saulus report section 2.7](https://theosaulus.fr/assets/pdf/Adversarial_ML_ProjectReport.pdf)
- demanghon/contree.ai documents a Rust double-dummy solver, optional PIMC dataset generator, and supervised bidding/value and card-play/policy training. It over-samples capots, belote, unusual hand shapes and endgames for synthetic labels. The design paper does not report a strength comparison that supports its state-of-the-art goal. [Source and training commands](https://github.com/demanghon/contree.ai) [Design](https://github.com/demanghon/contree.ai/blob/main/paper.md)
- Coincheur (since June 2026) publishes multiple AI difficulties, configurable contracts and scoring, and matched-deal challenges. Expert simulates possible finishes at configurable budgets; the cited pages document no strength tests. There is also a classic mode. [Coincheur](https://coincheur.fr/) [FAQ](https://coincheur.fr/faq-coincheur.html) [Origin](https://coincheur.fr/en/about-coincheur.html)
- DaCoinch's developer describes its bundled AI as basic. coinche.online is a separate playable product with live leaderboard and tournaments. The Reddit ISMCTS description in discussion about coinche.online is in a comment about another earlier project, so do not attribute that method to coinche.online. [DaCoinch](https://dacoinch.sourceforge.net/help.html) [Coinche.online](https://coinche.online/) [Thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/jeudeplateau/comments/1wp24db/coinche_belote_contr%C3%A9e_en_ligne_4_mois_apr%C3%A8s_vos/)

### Inferences

- Public implementation details and local comparators make Colver a stronger handoff for Contrée than the studies and products alone. The author-reported generations of improvement do not establish rank against independent experts.
- A learned joint posterior can encode auction implications and dependencies between hidden cards. Playgen's model card also exposes limits, including no public belote declaration input and considerable rejection of generated worlds, especially early. Good belief calibration and whole-match accuracy are distinct questions. [Playgen model card](https://huggingface.co/Avo-k/colver-playgen-v2)

### Gaps

- No standardized public Contrée/Coinche cross-engine evaluation or controlled expert comparison found. Coincheur has no public benchmark on the pages checked. Colver provides executable arena and some reported model comparisons, but aggregate comparisons and production GPU service are private.

## What has been achieved for Bulgarian Bridge-Belot?

### Takeaway

Nikolay Kostov's public C# BelotGameEngine has the strongest documented regional bot comparison found. Its richer September 30 hybrid improved play in mirrored matches and is still available as CreateMaster. The app subsequently assigned its Master setting to a weaker, more conventionally instructive hybrid.

### Cited Findings

- The MIT C# project (Bulgarian and English UI) offers SmartPlayer heuristics, a 100-ms ISMCTS reference, a neural policy, and multiple hybrids. The NuGet engine package does not include the bots, but the source tree has bot code, embedded model weights and a simulator. For Bulgarian rules and English API semantics see the engine README. [Source](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine) [Engine](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/tree/master/src/Belot.Engine) [Models and trainer results](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/NEURAL_NETWORK.md)
- Sept 30 human-style Master uses natural-bidding fine-tuned networks, learned ownership distributions, simulations of the network's top few options in the early tricks, and exact double-dummy solving from the last five tricks with weighted worlds. It breaks near ties with human card preferences and adjusts inferred card ownership from the partner's discards. The authors measured 13.1 ms/card average and p95 30.8 ms on their desktop. In mirrored whole-game validation it won 63.60% ±1.30 pp over 1,000 games vs 100-ms ISMCTS, 79.50% ±0.83 pp over 2,000 vs a port of the 2001 program Belot 2.06, and 93.65% ±0.53 pp over 2,000 vs SmartPlayer, errors given as one standard error across pairs. [Human-style validation and design](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HUMAN_PLAY.md#final-validation-september-30-fresh-seeds-otherwise-idle-machine)
- Self-play created a private bidding relay. About 30% of eligible older model suit bids held neither the jack nor nine of the named suit; a cheap clubs bid could mean a general strong hand. Fine-tuning with natural-bid restrictions made the agent easier to partner. New Master with the same Belot 2.06 partner on both teams beat the earlier relay-playing Master 64.78% ±0.64 pp in 4,000 games, and 64.65% ±0.89 pp over a fresh 2,000-game test. These are bot partners selected to mimic conventional bidding, not human subjects. Collaborating with a partner who knows the bot's private relay produces materially different results. [Partner tests](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HUMAN_PLAY.md#what-the-relay-costs-a-human-partner)
- Fast settings include a frozen search-free net measuring 11.3 µs/decision in self-play and scoring 49.5% ±2.2 pp vs 100-ms ISMCTS in 400 matches, and a 90-world endgame hybrid measured at 31.1 µs/card that in a separate ISMCTS test won 53.45% in 2,000 games (95% CI 51.605–55.295%). Different benchmarks and saved weights underlie those rows. [Neural results](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/NEURAL_NETWORK.md)
- The October 8 app Master was changed to learned bidding plus hand-written card heuristics plus exact late search for a more teachable style. The hybrid averages 0.83 ms per card, beats Belot 2.06 73.95% ±0.40 pp (10,000 mirrored) and SmartPlayer 89.2% (repo summary); it scores 34.33% ±1.66 pp vs the former human-style Master (600 games). A 53.0% vs ISMCTS row over 200 games is underpowered. This change overrides stale general README wording. [October update and results](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HEURISTIC_PLAYER.md)
- PlayBelote's deployed Bulgarian game has Monte Carlo bidding and card play using random feasible deals (60 samples per card and 40 per bid). Its 13,123 games and 180,362 rounds across 20 Swiss/elimination competitions concern internal bot types, not a comparison with Kostov's bots or human experts. The author's separate unreleased v2 uses up to 48 sampled worlds and exact minimax with two cards remaining. [Author's writeup](https://datuzzo.com/work/playbelote/)

### Inferences

- The choice between fastest adequate policy, highest mirrored bot-versus-bot score, compatibility with a stranger, and understandable human play is consequential. The Bulgarian repo quantifies this with same-partner cross-play, self-play comparisons and alternate difficulty configurations.
- The quality of older Belot 2.06 and SmartPlayer is an internal reference, not a basis to equate fitted Elo to human ranks.

### Gaps

- No published controlled matches against a representative Bulgarian human population. September 29 and September 30 and October 8 profiles differ, as do model/repo variant and runtime budgets. Avoid interpreting a single app difficulty label across versions.

## What does the independent Moldovan Belot evidence establish?

### Takeaway

MrUnBaiat's independent BelotAI has informative offline ablations and an observational live test on Moldova's belot.md, distinct from Bulgarian Belot. Live play shows competitive results in the sampled population without a statistically clear positive per-hand edge when the site's replacement bot is excluded.

### Cited Findings

- Recurrent shared-parameter Multi-Agent PPO uses centralized training with a privileged critic, and the inference-time actor only sees the legal information of its own seat. Network bids and plays through trick index 2 (first three tricks). The hybrid then samples joint feasible deals uniformly under known-card and void constraints, performs exact double-dummy in each, and optimizes game-point margin. Its internal swap-paired improvement over the same frozen policy without search was +1.270 ±0.197 game points/hand over 1,000 deals in the headline setup. In the platform-correct scoring the sampling gain had saturated by about 32 worlds (D128−D32 +0.031 ±0.264). The learned bidder was optional and measured +0.126 ±0.054 game points/hand in 12,000 deals against network bidders. The rules implement platform-specific scoring (melds/combination contest, capot, every third bolt), so distinguish offline simulator/scoring versions and game-point units from classic French card-point results. [Repository](https://github.com/MrUnBaiat/BelotAI) [Detailed results](https://github.com/MrUnBaiat/BelotAI/blob/master/docs/RESULTS.md)
- September 24 author snapshot covers 94 recordings, 77 sessions, 627 observed matches, and 3,875 scored hands. The site substituted its own timeout bot into one or more other seats in 410 hands. Across the remaining 3,465 hands the reported mean is +0.42 ±0.46 game points per hand (nominal 95% interval). This group includes the author's bot in two seats when partnered with its own second bot account (+0.71 ±0.59, n=2,053) and cases with a stranger teammate (+0.00 ±0.73, n=1,412). The README's shorthand “all four seats human” is misleading: absence of a platform replacement bot is the criterion. Separate match statistic: 219 wins in 384 finished matches, 57.0% ±5.0 pp, with no explicit claim that this subset excludes site bots or the same-author partner. Do not use 627 as the match-win denominator. The live sample is observational and not duplicate-paired; the readme's general claim about paired figures explicitly excludes these live observations in its separate discussion. The reported per-hand interval may be optimistic if dependent hands were treated as independent; source does not document clustering in README. [Live results and methodology](https://github.com/MrUnBaiat/BelotAI#what-live-play-has-measured)
- Offline MIT engine, heuristic baseline, solver, evaluation tooling, and training code are public and do not require an account. The neural checkpoints are not distributed, and replay logs stay out of the repo for privacy. Historical figures thus cannot be reproduced from the exact original checkpoint and live sample alone. Belot.md's official August 23, 2026 site rules prohibit unauthorized bots and restrict use of multiple accounts. The author-provided observational results are not a platform-endorsed benchmark. [Project quick start](https://github.com/MrUnBaiat/BelotAI#quick-start) [Moldovan platform rules](https://belot.md/en/reguli_new.php)

### Inferences

- The platform observations are among the few direct human comparisons found, but they cannot support a superhuman claim, particularly when the stranger-partner estimate is near zero and the strongest opposing human population is unspecified.
- Earlier full-deal search can lose to a trained policy because the weight and uncertainty in hidden deals differ by trick. This is consistent with strong projects searching selective early actions and applying exact solves later.

### Gaps

- No independent replication, public raw logs, original author checkpoint, opponent strength characterization, random partner assignment, confidence interval grouped by match/session, or unambiguously filter-matched whole-match statistic found.

## Which rules and components matter for Bulgarian and Belgian implementation?

### Takeaway

Build and test separate observable rule profiles for Bulgarian Bridge-Belot and one named Belgian house ruleset. Their search machinery can share a core, while auctions, public declarations, legal move generation and scores feed different information into it.

### Cited Findings

- Bulgaria's Belot.BG rules use a partial hand for the ranked auction (clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades, no trump, all trump), then complete the deal. Opponents can double and the declaring side can redouble. No trump uses follow-suit only, suppresses declarations and doubles raw points before rounding, except the capot bonus. All trump forces going higher when following suit and possible, even if a partner leads. The game target is 151, with tie carry and special rounding. Card-point totals with last trick before any melds or capot are 162 (suit), 260 (no trump after doubling), 258 (all trump). [Bulgarian rules](https://belot.bg/belot/rules/)
- The Bulgarian engine API uses seeded BelotMatch, exposes a seat-scoped view, and accepts separate Bid, Announce and PlayCard actions. Validate checks invalid actions without mutating the match; accepted moves advance forced steps. The open source repo's validation reports 741 engine tests; bots and neural weights are source-distributed and absent from the NuGet engine package. [C# engine readme](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/src/Belot.Engine/README.md) [Source and MIT license](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine) [Test report](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/NEURAL_NETWORK.md#final-v2-app-calibration)
- Belote-Rebelote.be documents a Belgian version with two à-la-retourne rounds, announced melds, 101 reduced points with a minimum two-point margin, inviolable belote and 81–81 carryover. The RJCV Belgian tournament uses a forced third auction round, 101 points, rounding up from 6, announcement on first trick and showing on second, no shared cards between declarations, King-first belote, 81–81 carry, and explicit contextual “pisser” obligations. Labelote.be, by contrast, advertises classic 501 and its own coinchée 701. [Belgian version](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/regles-belote) [Reduced scoring](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/compter-points-belote) [RJCV tournament rules](https://belote.rjcv.be/regles.pdf) [Labelote.be FAQ](https://labelote.be/faq)

### Inferences

- Keep a shared 32-card representation, deterministic history/replay, game-limited seat observations, world-sampler interface, alpha-beta/rollout infrastructure, paired evaluation and testing framework. Rule profiles need contract-specific rank and legal follow policy, dealing/disclosure and auction decisions, announcement timing/eligibility, score settlement/rounding/carryover and match wins. Legality and scoring should be called by the solver and sampler so those modules cannot learn alternate house rules. Share policy tooling and feature design, but evaluate and train variant-specific bidding and policies on their own rewards.
- Golden positions should exercise no-trump follow-only and all-trump overtake, partner-winner exceptions and overtrump/no-higher branches, matched/invalid meld or belote announcements, exactly tied declarations/contract and next-deal carry, capot/double/redouble and reduced-score rounding, plus the Belgian forced round and King-first cases if the RJCV profile is selected. A fast double-dummy solver should agree with exhaustive enumeration late in both variants. Hidden-world samples must assign all remaining cards once, respect known cards and legal public action history, and agree with exact small-state enumeration.

### Gaps

- No Belgian-wide uniform rules or quantitative bot benchmark under the exact chosen Belgian house rules emerged. The 101-to-win rulesets still differ in auction completion and undertrumping obligations. Current user request identifies two regions but no precise Belgian table rulebook.
