# Transfer from trick-taking AI and verification of Colver

## What methods have actually transferred across trick-taking games?

### Takeaway

Sampling legal hidden deals and searching or rolling out each deal is a strong practical starting point. Models of observed bidding/play can improve search, but better prediction of hidden cards has yielded uneven playing gains, and exact play within a fully revealed world still models knowledge players do not actually have.

### Cited Findings

- PIMC uses the current seat's information to generate plausible full deals, solves each as a perfect-information game, then aggregates. Strategy fusion arises when future choices are implicitly allowed to depend on a hidden deal the actor cannot distinguish. Nonlocality arises when other players steer play based on information we lack, changing which worlds are plausible at a position. More samples alone cannot remove these structural errors. [Long et al., AAAI 2010](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/aaai10-mc.pdf)
- ISMCTS uses determinizations to build a shared information-set tree, or one tree per player in the multiple-observer variant, pooling search effort and addressing the standard fusion pathology under the relevant information-set representation. Its original authors identify opponent modelling and beliefs as separate tools needed for nonlocality. [Cowling, Powley and Whitehouse, IEEE 2012](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf)
- Jass Schieber research (Niklaus et al., 2020) found determinized MCTS and a supervised DNN similarly strong, ahead of ISMCTS, the random baseline, and a robust rule bot in their cardplay comparison. Their learned card-distribution estimates did not materially improve the matching search variants. They used a Swisslos corpus of about 1.8M rounds, swapped team hands in paired rounds, and evaluated trump selection and play separately. Code and tournament server links are in the paper. [Niklaus et al., Schieber Jass, 2020](https://rlg.mlanctot.info/papers/AAAI20-RLG_paper_21.pdf)
- Skat's Kermit improved when sampled worlds were weighted by the probability of the observed play under learned human bidding and card policies. Its policy-based inference beat a neural marginal-card baseline by 2.32 tournament points per suit game and 1.57 per null game. The 0.64 estimate in grand was not significant. The controlled experiment held the auction/declaration fixed, tested 5,000 paired matches per game type, and swapped solo/defense roles. Another Kermit experiment even found the true-world-only evaluator could perform worse than a sampled-world one in suit and grand, so a double-dummy result is exact for the fully visible game but need not predict the best actual choice or be a universal upper bound on realized performance. [Rebstock et al., IEEE CoG 2019, Table III and IV](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/cog2019-policy-based-inference.pdf)
- Separately, in Skat, imitation learning from human games improved bidding and declaration, while the learned play policy was a little weaker than the strongest search bot and orders of magnitude faster. A 2025 author preprint reports mixing human expert and synthetic self-play data to improve predictions for early choices. [Rebstock, Solinas and Buro, 2019](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/aaai2019RLWS-learning-policies-from-human-data.pdf) [Edelkamp, 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15435)
- Bridge's alpha-mu keeps outcome vectors across worlds rather than immediately averaging each action's full-information outcome. It is a more expensive option for testing information-consistent endgames, and its Bridge formulation assumes defenders know declarer's cards. In the 2022 NooK human benchmark, eight world champions and NooK each played identical hands in the declarer role on fixed 3NT contracts with the same Wbridge5 robot defenders, a well-controlled test of a single part of bridge. [Cazenave and Ventos, alpha-mu](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cazenave/papers/AlphaMu.pdf) [University account of NooK test](https://www.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/en/news/top-stories/science-and-innovation/the-conversation/sciences/the-conversation-an-ai-bridge-champion-1095848.kjsp?RH=1653310265592)
- Closely related Dutch Klaverjas thesis found single-observer ISMCTS with random rollouts beat rule, human-data value, and self-play value approaches at a high search budget. At 200 simulations, head-to-head, rollouts were +3.9 points versus human-data and +13.1 versus self-play (SE 1.3 over 10,000 rounds each). Low-budget results favored networks, with high-budget performance and wall-clock comparisons better to check directly. [Bart Aaldering, Leiden University thesis, §4 and §7](https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2022-2023-AalderingBBart.pdf)
- Colver's neural-play inspiration is DouZero, a 2021 ICML deep Monte Carlo self-play system with a strong benchmark in DouDizhu, a climbing game with different action and team structures. [Zha et al., ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zha21a.html)
- Direct research predating Serrao: Edelkamp's KI 2024 conference paper generalized a Skat-style trick-taking framework to multiple games including Belote, with game/contract selection and general versus expert-rule card players. [Springer published abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-70893-0_6) [Conference abstract](https://www.informatik.uni-wuerzburg.de/ki24/program/abstracts-of-accepted-papers/)
- The commercial Dix-de-Der mobile app's own guide documents classic Belote and Coinche plus a hand-coded Tree bot and a downloaded self-play Neural bot that can work offline. Its Expert single-player rating replays the identical deal with AI in the user's seat. Developer describes the bot as beatable by a good human, rather than publishing a controlled external strength measurement. [Dix-de-Der official rules and AI documentation](https://dixdeder.fr/regles/?lang=en)

### Inferences

- For a strong Belote implementation, freeze a ruleset, enforce own-information access and rules-derived void/overtrump constraints, compare a rules-only policy against shared-world rollout or PIMC and ISMCTS, and use a contract-aware exact endgame where affordable. Any learned posterior should compete against a hard-constraints-only sampler at equal wall-clock and be measured both by held-out calibration and downstream strength. Jointly valid sampled hands matter more for search than independent card marginals. [PIMC analysis](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/aaai10-mc.pdf) [Kermit controlled evaluation](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/cog2019-policy-based-inference.pdf)
- Benchmark separate bidding-only and play-only swaps plus full match play. Pair identical deals with swapped teams and rotate seats/dealer, hold out complete deals, report paired confidence intervals, full match win rate and deal score difference, with latency at fixed hardware and a predeclared world/time budget. Run mixed partnerships as well as full bot teams to catch convention compatibility; human ladder aggregates are informative but cannot substitute for duplicate testing against known strong humans. [Jass duplicate rounds](https://rlg.mlanctot.info/papers/AAAI20-RLG_paper_21.pdf) [Kermit mixed-role evaluation](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/cog2019-policy-based-inference.pdf) [Colver historical arena protocol](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/v0.8.0/docs/arena_results.md)

### Gaps

- Edelkamp's chapter is paywalled and I did not verify its exact Belote rules, per-game benchmark table, implementation download or head-to-head strength. [Publisher preview](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-70893-0_6)
- No independently controlled competitive standard spanning classic Belote, Contrée and regional Belot surfaced. A Belgian-hosted 2020 Université de Liège programming assignment describes a referee that receives the trump and taking team as inputs. It explicitly excludes negotiation details and evaluates legality and scoring, so it offers no evidence about Belgian bot strength or bidding conventions. Belgian authorship of an experiment alone does not establish that the experiment targets local Belgian rules. Dix-de-Der does not disclose enough method detail for independent cross-bot comparison in its rules documentation. [Liège assignment](https://people.montefiore.uliege.be/info0004/files/oopp-project1-brief.pdf) [Dix-de-Der documentation](https://dixdeder.fr/regles/?lang=en)

## How reproducible are Colver's recent Contrée agents and results?

### Takeaway

Colver exposes a live app, MIT engine and bot arena, model weights and historical training code. Its statistical evidence is developer-run and mostly compares components or earlier internal models. Where its historical and current documentation quote different numbers, protocol details explain some differences and warrant explicit attribution.

### Cited Findings

- Latest GitHub tagged release I verified is v0.8.0 (2026-07-23); the newer PyPI wheel v0.11.1 is dated 2026-08-07. The current master tree 244e842… includes the core Rust DD/IS-DD and Playgen inference, arena and four published TOML specs. It lacks training binaries despite README mentioning `train_joint`. The earlier v0.8.0 tag still publicly exposes historical `train_joint`, `train_dmc`, `train_bid_nn` and `train_playgen` binaries. The website and full results are excluded from the current public source by the author. [GitHub v0.8.0](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/releases/tag/v0.8.0) [PyPI 0.11.1](https://pypi.org/project/colver/0.11.1/) [Current master tree](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/tree/244e842147296ff8349f8653276a6fa73c779e73) [Historical training source](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/tree/v0.8.0/colver-core/src/bin)
- Direct Hugging Face weights URLs returned HTTP 401 in this session. Public GitHub Releases API verifies fallback weights named `dmc_50.bin` in v0.4.0, `bid_v6_isdd.bin` in v0.7.0, and `playgen_v2_final.bin` in v0.8.0. The model cards are mirrored in repo docs, so use these links for reproducibility. [DouDou50 release](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/releases/tag/v0.4.0) [Bid V6 release](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/releases/tag/v0.7.0) [Playgen release](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/releases/tag/v0.8.0) [Model documentation](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/tree/master/docs/hf)
- The two live options share the score-aware V6 bidder, trained with self-play bidding and results from an IS-DD-labelled pool. DouDou50 is fast CPU inference (~0.5 ms in model card); Dédé uses Playgen's sampled hands and DD per world (1 second browser target, model card quotes 1.2 s). DouDou50 model card reports it loses clearly to IS-DD in direct play, with no numerical margin. The older v0.4 release called DouDou50 equal to its then-current IS-DD solver. Do not mix these claims. [Current DouDou50 model card](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/doudou50/README.md) [Current bots](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver#comparaison-des-agents)
- V6 versus V5 in paired 2,000-point matches had 55.8% wins with DouDou50 play or 57.3% and +181 points per match with IS-DD. The separate 58.7%/+140 row in the v0.7.0 release is a three-bot DMC-play round-robin standing, as the historical arena docs explain. Same 57.3% row is described as 500 in the historical arena doc and 1,000 in the release; possibly count per direction versus combined, so omit sample size or explicitly attribute it to release. The same deck scoring can strongly favor a different model with per-deal utility: release reports V6 −22 points per deal versus V5 in its standalone audit even while winning more full matches. [Bid V6 model card](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/bid-v6/README.md) [v0.7.0 release](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/releases/tag/v0.7.0) [Historical arena table](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/v0.8.0/docs/arena_results.md)
- v0.8.0 historical arena documents that an older card-location belief net, holding Bid V6 and IS-DD constant, achieved 49.6% and −8 points per match in 1,000 matches, while the earlier Bid V2 configuration gained about 13 percentage points. This is not the current Playgen transformer. Playgen masks hard constraints, uses the private observer's known cards, and completes sampled hidden histories. Model card reports ~10 valid worlds per 100 attempted at trick one, no explicit belote announcement input, and a private ~50× faster GPU deployment sidecar; the same inference is available on CPU via the published weights and Rust sampler. Its auction head achieved 48.2% vs Bid V6 over 3,000 matches, a separate result from the IS-DD play-stage ablation. [Historical arena belief ablation](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/v0.8.0/docs/arena_results.md) [Playgen model card](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/playgen-v2/README.md)
- Live rules page describes the exact Colver variant; current model cards note the 2026-08-02 rule fix changed 0.014% of measured actual decisions in their corpus. Contract policies or comparisons should never be blindly carried from Contrée into classic à la retourne, no-trump/all-trump Coinche, or regional Belot. [Colver rules](https://colver.net/regles) [Bid V6 model card](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/bid-v6/README.md)

### Inferences

- Colver is an unusually complete publicly inspectable Contrée baseline. One can reproduce CPU policies and an arena, but should verify actual weight versions, package/tag, selected rules and time budget before claiming parity with the deployed GPU bot. [Current repo](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver) [Model cards](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/tree/master/docs/hf)
- For Belgian and Bulgarian implementations, the transferable parts are card bitsets, alpha-beta with transposition tables, sampling over legal hidden hands, root rollouts, and the evaluation harness. The hard constraints, trump ranks, auction policy, announcements, terminal utility and any trained model need ruleset-specific validation or retraining. This follows directly from how Colver bakes fixed auction actions and scoring into the model and environment. [Colver rules and implementation](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver) [Colver bidder encoding](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/bid-v6/README.md)
- The score-aware bidder's full-match improvements illustrate why optimizing a deal's expected score is not always the same as optimizing the chance of winning a match. [v0.7.0 release](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/releases/tag/v0.7.0)

### Gaps

- No independent comparison against strong human Contrée partnerships or third-party bots under identical rules found. The Colver rankings update by deal for accounts and bot types, which is different from such a comparison. [Colver site](https://colver.net) [Colver repo](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver)
- No public current fixed-time ablation quantifying Playgen worlds versus uniform worlds downstream under the actual Dédé deployment, and no numeric DouDou50 versus current Dédé match result confirmed. [Playgen model card](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/playgen-v2/README.md) [DouDou50 model card](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/master/docs/hf/doudou50/README.md)
