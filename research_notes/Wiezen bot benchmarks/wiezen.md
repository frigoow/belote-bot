# Ordinary Wiezen bot evidence

## Are there playable bots or public measured results?

### Takeaway

Whisthub provides a playable bot on near-IWWA ordinary Wiezen. WildWally-Codes/boomwies is the most advanced technical public implementation I found for this variant, with Monte Carlo card play, simulated bidding for selected contracts, exact late-game search and a duplicate-deal harness. I found no publicly reported bot-strength results under ordinary Wiezen rules.

### Cited Findings

- **Whisthub ordinary Wiezen.** Free solo versus three computer players or mixed human/bot tables. Whisthub describes its rules and scores as matching IWWA except that a dealer playing miserie cannot exchange the turned card, so that card remains known to opponents. Its founder says Wiezen launched in September 2021 and reused nearly all Colour Whist logic, particularly the AI. The FAQ describes a teaching/pleasant-play goal and acknowledges bot moves can look odd. [Ordinary Wiezen lobby](https://www.whisthub.com/nl/wiezen/lobby) · [Founder’s history](https://www.whisthub.com/blog/the-history-of-whisthub) · [Whisthub FAQ](https://www.whisthub.com/nl/faq)
- **WildWally-Codes/boomwies.** Public JavaScript implementation for ordinary Wiezen, Boomkewies and Classic Boomke, with live solo/mixed play. The card player compares legal cards across sampled unseen deals, using inferred voids, known cards and hand-written bid likelihood weights at higher levels; the sampler drops void constraints after 12 failed attempts, so this is not strictly consistent when its fallback is reached. The policy distinguishes taking, avoidance and forcing a miserie declarer to take. Master has 220 nominal card-play worlds and a 1,000 ms loop cap, caps exact double-dummy evaluations at 50 samples once the acting hand has three or fewer cards, and uses 48 bid samples. Actual counts can be smaller. It simulates outcomes for selected solo and miserie bids and preserves the dealer’s turned card in bid samples. Beginner is a no-rollout baseline with injected errors, Club uses a nominal 60 samples. These numbers are settings, not performance measurements. [Repo](https://github.com/WildWally-Codes/boomwies) · [AI](https://github.com/WildWally-Codes/boomwies/blob/main/src/ai/index.js) · [Settings](https://github.com/WildWally-Codes/boomwies/blob/main/src/ai/config.js) · [Bid simulations](https://github.com/WildWally-Codes/boomwies/blob/main/src/ai/bidsim.js)
- Boomwies includes a CLI arena. `node scripts/tournament.js wiezen master 100` plays each deck seed four times with the candidate in each seat and three Club bots; output is the candidate’s signed points per hand under that app's scoring. The source uses seeded initial deals but calls global stochastic policy functions, so runs need not yield identical results. Tests include full games and scoring invariants. No author-published strength table or explicit license was found in the public repo. [Arena](https://github.com/WildWally-Codes/boomwies/blob/main/scripts/tournament.js) · [Game tests](https://github.com/WildWally-Codes/boomwies/blob/main/tests/engine.test.js)
- **mark-vandenbroeck/Wiezen.** Public Flask implementation of ordinary upcard Wiezen. Plays against three selectable-difficulty computer opponents. Its Hard level uses hand rules plus card counting and void tracking; the unit tests cover tactical choices such as following suit cheaply and overtrumping. These are behavior checks, with no strength results or explicit license found. [Repo](https://github.com/mark-vandenbroeck/Wiezen) · [AI source](https://github.com/mark-vandenbroeck/Wiezen/blob/HEAD/ai_player.py) · [Tests](https://github.com/mark-vandenbroeck/Wiezen/blob/HEAD/tests/test_ai_strategy.py)
- **Other ordinary Wiezen code/playables.** Jan Van Wassenhove’s browser app mITy.John Cards offers three bot levels, the ordinary Wiezen bid ladder and regional settings such as 8/9 for Troel. Apache-2.0 `toverdraak/Wiezen` gives Java trick-position heuristics with random alternatives. WolfRogerMatthias/Wiezen is an early Python hand-evaluation/playing baseline. No published strength figures found for any of these. [Cards](https://mityjohn.com/apps/cards/) · [Java bot source](https://github.com/toverdraak/Wiezen/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/merlijnsmislukkingen/wiezen/Bot.java) · [Python prototype](https://github.com/WolfRogerMatthias/Wiezen/blob/HEAD/src/Game/Players/BaselinePlayer.py)
- Whisthub's detailed source note on related AI describes simple ranking of cards as an illustrative fragment and a play-only-legal-card shortcut. The cited 200,000 AI self-play games checked legality for **Kingen** after a bug fix. This is neither a Wiezen/Kleurenwiezen test nor a result on strength. [Whisthub Kingen technical note](https://www.whisthub.com/blog/the-kingen-ai-has-been-cheating)

### Inferences

- For a build, start with a named rule profile, deterministic replay, legal-move validation on every simulated action and a card-counting heuristic. Test score-based Monte Carlo play and bidding separately before a late exact solver. Keep deck seed, dealer and candidate seat fixed in the comparison. Report per-seat paired points, contract success by bid and latency; the 200,000 Kingen simulations cannot be used as a win-rate claim.
- A one-versus-three Club arena is useful and follows individual scoring. A mixed table with multiple baseline styles would probe robustness better than a single fixed opposition.

### Gaps

- Multilingual general web, academic index and GitHub searches surfaced direct code, but no published win rate, score gain, strength ranking or human evaluation for exact ordinary Wiezen. Whisthub's complete AI and strength data are undisclosed. One author of an allWhist Android app claims a Ghent Whist variant with difficulty settings via a test recruitment post, but its store release could not be verified and adds little to stronger confirmed examples.
- A repo titled Whist or Solo Whist alone does not verify the rules. English classic Whist uses fixed partners and no bidding; British Solo Whist is related but has distinct scoring. [Whisthub ordinary Wiezen FAQ](https://www.whisthub.com/wiezen/lobby) · [Classic Whist](https://www.pagat.com/whist/whist.html)
- Scorekeepers such as Dikdikdik and WieZcore cover scoring and do not supply computer opponents. [Dikdikdik developer post](https://blog.johanv.org/en/posts/wiezen-score-app/) · [WieZcore app listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.kvk.wiezcoreapp)

## Which rules affect comparative bot claims?

### Takeaway

Choose an explicit version for each game. Ordinary Wiezen's last card sets the default trump and supplies ownership information before play; Kleurenwiezen normally chooses trump in the auction.

### Cited Findings

- IWWA's ordinary Wiezen tournament rules support a 52-card game, Vragen/Meegaan for eight tricks in the turned suit, five solo if no partner, and higher contracts such as Miserie, Abondance, Open Miserie, Solo and Solo Slim. Troel can designate the missing ace's holder as partner and set trump. Ordinary IWWA results sum to zero across four seats. The primary PDF's 2020 version date is printed in the file. Other organized Belgian clubs publish distinct rules, including Rijk van Afdronk's documentation of differences with IWWA scoring. [IWWA Wiezen rules](https://www.whistiwwa.com/_files/ugd/d0d088_95f7e3fb8ff34d4babf43229b7a4d8c4.pdf) · [Rijk van Afdronk rules](https://rijkvanafdronk.be/wiezen/) · [Scoring comparison](https://rijkvanafdronk.be/blog/hoeveel-voor-een-overslag/)
- IWWA Kleurenwiezen's rulebook sets six-trick minimum solo, requires shuffling after every hand and has payoffs that need not sum to zero. Whisthub Kleurenwiezen's profile permits five-trick solo, describes cutting only and all scores summing to zero. These can make fixed scoring and hidden-hand inference behave differently. [IWWA Colour Whist rules](https://www.whistiwwa.com/_files/ugd/d0d088_65a0d59965024bf6bc80c960f161094d.pdf) · [Whisthub Colour Whist rules](https://www.whisthub.com/nl/rules)
- GinoVanHoudt/wiezen-v2 is **Kleurenwiezen** despite the repo name. Its public TypeScript bot estimates tricks and miserie risk heuristically, conditions on card history and chooses engine-legal actions; no measured results or explicit license found. Dewaeq/kleuren-wiezen is a Rust play prototype with RandomPlayer and GreedyPlayer. The default 800,000 samples per homogeneous lineup time 13 tricks and print games/s, with no head-to-head strength or full auction. [GinoVanHoudt repo](https://github.com/GinoVanHoudt/wiezen-v2) · [Bot source](https://github.com/GinoVanHoudt/wiezen-v2/blob/main/src/game/bot.ts) · [Dewaeq throughput harness](https://github.com/Dewaeq/kleuren-wiezen/blob/HEAD/src/bench.rs)

### Inferences

- Reusing deck representation, legality interface, sampling and arena from another trick-taker is sensible. Reward should reflect the contract's actual seat payoff and which seats are currently allied. Assumptions of always teaming opposite seats, always striving for the most tricks, or always using zero-sum match utility fail across profiles.
- The 52-card game has 13 decisions per hand before bidding and a much larger initial deal space than 32-card Belote. Measure early-play search budgets separately from exact late-game search and encode whether previous deals were cut or fully shuffled if modeling a cut-only tournament profile.

### Gaps

- No uniform house rules under names including Belgian Whist, Whist à la couleur, and Wiezen. Whist-Belgique's promotional page uses a broad Wiezen label but lists Piccolo and advertises experts without quantitative tests; classify it from its game rules if used as a benchmark. [Whist-Belgique](https://www.whist-belgique.be/wiezen-online)
