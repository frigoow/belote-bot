# Building Bulgarian and Belgian Belote bots

*Research snapshot, 21 November 2026*

**Bulgarian Bridge-Belot has a reusable open-source engine and a strong, measured bot. Belgian Belote has documented local rules and several nearby AI benchmarks, with no public quantitative bot benchmark found under the documented Belgian rules.** Nikolay Kostov’s MIT-licensed BelotGameEngine is the starting point for Bulgarian play. For Belgium, start with general classic upcard Belote, also called Belote à la retourne, and use the 101-point Belgian scoring documented by Belote-Rebelote.be as a provisional default. Adapt the strongest straightforward search technique demonstrated on classic play. Sample legal hidden deals and compare cards by simulating the rest of the hand. Both profiles can share search and evaluation infrastructure. The strongest published numbers below come from author-run experiments, and we did not rerun the matches. [BelotGameEngine](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine) · [Belgian classic rules](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/regles-belote) · [Belgian scoring](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/compter-points-belote) · [Pemeja’s classic Belote experiments](https://engineering.kaseovo.com/projects/belote-ai/)

## Bulgarian Bridge-Belot has a runnable reference

**Kostov’s September 30 `CreateMaster` hybrid won 63.60% ±1.30 percentage points against the project’s 100 ms ISMCTS reference over 1,000 mirrored games.** It won 79.50% ±0.83 points against a port of the 2001 Belot 2.06 program and 93.65% ±0.53 against the project’s SmartPlayer, each over 2,000 mirrored games. These uncertainty figures are the author’s standard errors across pairs. The hybrid averages 13.1 ms per card with a 30.8 ms 95th percentile on the author’s desktop. It uses learned bidding and card policies, simulates a shortlist of early cards, and applies exact double-dummy search from the last five tricks over plausible hidden deals. The source tree includes bots, embedded weights and simulation tools. The NuGet engine package contains the rules engine. The app’s October 8 Master label points to a different, faster configuration with hand-written card-play rules and exact late search. [September 30 validation and architecture](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HUMAN_PLAY.md#final-validation-september-30-fresh-seeds-otherwise-idle-machine) · [October 8 configuration](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HEURISTIC_PLAYER.md) · [Source and license](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine)

Partnership compatibility already changes the ranking. Early self-play learned a private bidding relay, including cheap suit bids used to convey general strength. With the same conventional Belot 2.06 partner on both sides, the natural-bidding hybrid won **64.78% ±0.64 points** against the older relay-playing Master across 4,000 mirrored games. That is controlled cross-play with a conventional bot partner. Human partnership strength remains unmeasured in this benchmark. A live observational comparison from Moldova’s separate BelotAI project found a mean of +0.00 ±0.73 game points per hand with a stranger partner over 1,412 hands after excluding hands with the platform’s replacement bot. Public evidence therefore leaves expert human strength open for both target variants. [Bulgarian cross-play](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HUMAN_PLAY.md#what-the-relay-costs-a-human-partner) · [Moldovan BelotAI live results](https://github.com/MrUnBaiat/BelotAI#what-live-play-has-measured)

For a Bulgarian implementation, anchor the rules to Belot.BG and check engine behavior on the exact profile. The auction starts with five cards per player and ranks the four suit contracts, no trump, and all trump. The remaining cards arrive after bidding. The rules cover contra and recontra, follow-suit-only no trump, mandatory heading when following suit in all trump, declarations, contract settlement and rounding to a 151-point target. Kostov’s seeded match and seat-scoped view give the rules engine and bot a clean interface. [Belot.BG rules](https://belot.bg/belot/rules/) · [Engine API](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/src/Belot.Engine/README.md)

## Classic upcard Belote is the Belgian starting point

**The general classic rules give the Belgian bot its core game.** Four players in fixed partnerships get five cards each and see one upcard. The first bidding round offers its suit, the second offers the other suits, and eight passes trigger a redeal. Once a player takes, everyone completes their hand to eight cards. Players follow suit, and trump obligations depend on who is winning the trick. For the first Belgian build, use the Belgian web app's published scoring profile as a provisional default. Its reduced score targets 101 with a two-point lead, carries the taker's points on an 81–81 tie, and supports optional melds. The app offers bots, with no published strength measurements or bot source found. [Belgian classic rules](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/regles-belote) · [Belgian scoring](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/compter-points-belote) · [Belgian web app](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/)

Belgian tournament rules document optional profiles for later support. Reumonjoie forces a suit choice after two rounds of passes once the designated player has eight cards, applies additional trump obligations, and requires King-first belote. Tilff combines forced choice and King-first belote with raw scoring across four blocks of 12 deals, an 81–81 split and no other melds. Temploux uses a 101-point target and leaves out the forced third round. The Liège engineering students’ rules include no-trump and all-trump overcalls and a forced take on an upcard jack. These examples come from francophone Belgian organizers. [Reumonjoie](https://belote.rjcv.be/reglement2022.pdf) · [Tilff 2026](https://www.act-tilff.be/reglement-belote) · [Temploux](https://www.basketclubs.be/abctemploux/news/tournoi-de-belotte-2019/) · [Liège student rules, pp. 56–59](https://aees.be/media/ingenus/pdfs/Ingenu_de_rentree_2025__Copy__compressed.pdf)

## Classic Belote experiments favor a practical search baseline

Tanguy Pemeja’s closest applicable benchmark uses French classic à la retourne with a redeal after eight passes, no general melds and a raw single-deal margin. His search considers each legal card on the same sampled deals and finishes each with a hand-written policy. **With bidding fixed, 32 sampled worlds gained 38.68 points per deal over that heuristic alone** (95% CI 35.18–42.19) at 114.2 ms per deal on the reported setup. Four worlds gained 26.03 points per deal at 14.5 ms. At fixed bidding, the distilled PlayNet trailed Monte Carlo by 5.17 points per deal. Pairing PlayNet with the improved BidNet brought the combined bot within 0.63 points per deal of Monte Carlo with rule bidding over 6,000 deals (95% CI for the difference −2.06 to +0.81), with a reported 148× speedup. The headline speed comparison therefore includes better bidding on the faster side. The simulator fork is public, and the linked agent repository returned 404 when checked. [Pemeja’s results and protocol](https://engineering.kaseovo.com/projects/belote-ai/) · [Public OpenSpiel simulator fork](https://github.com/tpemeja/open_spiel/tree/belote-cpp-rework)

Alexandre Serrao’s UCLouvain thesis tested its own upcard variant, including all-trump and no-trump options and forced choice after two rounds of passes. The choice comes before the final three cards are dealt in the thesis and after they are dealt at Reumonjoie. Its online outcome-sampling and external-sampling Monte Carlo counterfactual regret minimization agents won **84% and 81.5%** against its ISMCTS baseline. Both averaged **40 seconds per move**, versus **3 seconds** for ISMCTS, and the headline table gives neither sample sizes nor intervals. Deep CFR produced roughly 45–60% against random across tested settings. These results motivate a higher-compute research comparison, while Pemeja supplies an interactive baseline and a cleaner bidding-versus-play breakdown. French Contrée’s MIT-licensed Colver is an additional implementation reference for constrained hidden-hand sampling, double-dummy search and an arena. Its bidding and scoring belong to its own four-suit numerical-contract variant. [Serrao thesis, §§4–6](https://perso.uclouvain.be/eric.piette/master/2025%20-%20Performance%20Evaluation%20of%20Counterfactual%20Regret%20Minimization%20and%20Monte%20Carlo%20Tree%20Search%20Variants%20-%20A%20Case%20Study%20on%20Belote.pdf) · [Colver source](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver) · [Colver rules](https://colver.net/regles)

## Share the search infrastructure and measure strength per profile

Share card encoding, seeded replay, seat-scoped observations, the hidden-hand sampler interface, rollout and endgame search, and the evaluation harness. Each rules profile should own the deal and auction, card ranks and legality, announcements, score settlement and match termination. Use the same rule functions in gameplay and simulation. That prevents a simulated player from learning from a move it could never legally see. Start with reproducible test positions for Bulgarian no-trump and all-trump, doubling and rounding, and Belgian two-round bidding, redeals, trump obligations, melds and tie carry. Add fixtures for forced choice and King-first belote when enabling a corresponding tournament profile. Verify the endgame solver and sampler against exhaustive enumeration on small positions. [Bulgarian engine API](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/src/Belot.Engine/README.md) · [Belgian classic rules](https://www.belote-rebelote.be/regles-belote) · [Colver solver](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/tree/master/colver-core/src/search)

For Belgian play, establish a conventional bidder and card heuristic, then test legal-world rollouts using the same sampled hands for each candidate card. Add exact double-dummy solving when sufficiently few cards remain and compare with ISMCTS at the same wall-clock budget. Exact double-dummy positions assume all hands are visible, so a sequence that works in a single sampled deal can rely on information the bot will never receive. Treat bid and discard likelihoods as an optional learned improvement once the hard-constraint sampler works. Preserve joint card assignments and compare the learned sampler against uniform legal sampling at the same time budget. [Analysis of determinization](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/aaai10-mc.pdf) · [Original ISMCTS paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf) · [Skat belief-model experiments](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/cog2019-policy-based-inference.pdf)

Use duplicate deals, swap team assignments, and rotate seats and the dealer. Measure bidding with the same card player and card play with the same bidder or fixed contract. Report match win rates, paired score margins with uncertainty, and decision latency on a specified machine. Include mixed partnerships early if the bot will fill a seat alongside a person. An all-bot team can score well by agreeing on bids that a human partner reads differently, and a change that wins more complete matches can still lower average score per deal. [Kostov’s mixed-partner comparison](https://github.com/NikolayIT/BelotGameEngine/blob/master/HUMAN_PLAY.md) · [Colver arena protocol and match scoring](https://github.com/Avo-k/colver/blob/v0.8.0/docs/arena_results.md) · [Jass paired evaluation](https://rlg.mlanctot.info/papers/AAAI20-RLG_paper_21.pdf)

## Conclusion

Record the rulebook and its version alongside every game, model and benchmark. A single local rule can change both what a discard reveals and which team receives the points. Versioning those rules lets a future Belgian club profile reuse the search machinery while preserving a reproducible benchmark for the initial classic profile.
